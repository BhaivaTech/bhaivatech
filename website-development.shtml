What can be scoped into a website project

The final scope depends on the business, content, integrations, and responsibilities agreed during discovery. A project can be considered around the following building blocks:

Business and marketing websites

A focused website can explain what a business does, who it serves, why a visitor should trust it, and what to do next. Typical planning questions include the primary service, target customer, service area, proof that may be published, enquiry route, and the content the client will supply.

Responsive front-end experience

Bhaivatech lists Next.js, React and plain HTML/CSS among its options. The right fit depends on the website’s requirements.

Search-ready foundations

The page can be planned with a clear page purpose, descriptive headings, useful copy, crawlable internal links, and a sensible enquiry path. “Search-ready” does not mean guaranteed rankings, AI recommendations, traffic, or leads. Search visibility depends on the finished implementation, competition, relevance, accessibility, and many factors outside a page draft.

E-commerce requirements

An online store needs more than a product grid. Before approval, define products and variants, catalogue ownership, payments, taxes where relevant, shipping or fulfilment, customer notifications, returns, third-party accounts, security responsibilities, and ongoing maintenance. E-commerce is therefore a scoping path, not an automatic inclusion in every website project.

UI direction and conversion paths

The current public portfolio shows brand-matched visual directions, focused summaries, and enquiry-oriented calls to action. A new project should define the audience, tone, priority action, content hierarchy, and any required booking, contact, or WhatsApp handoff before visual decisions are finalised.

Hosting, domain, and deployment questions

Bhaivatech lists AWS deployment, cPanel hosting, domain setup, SSL certificates and server management among its services. Ask the proposal to specify which apply, who owns the accounts and what renewals cost.

What is not automatically included

Unless explicitly written into an approved proposal, do not assume that a website project includes:

  • a fixed number of pages, revisions, products, integrations, or languages;
  • copywriting, photography, illustration, video, testimonials, or client logos;
  • paid plugins, themes, hosting, domains, email, payment gateways, or other third-party fees;
  • ongoing SEO, AEO, content publishing, link building, analytics repair, or reporting;
  • automation, AI-agent workflows, CRM work, or WhatsApp API implementation;
  • indefinite maintenance, emergency support, guaranteed response times, or guaranteed results;
  • migration, historical data cleanup, or recovery of an unavailable prior website.

A practical proposed process

  1. Initial conversation: Share the business, current website (if any), target customer, desired outcome, service area, and the most important action a visitor should take.
  2. Scope discovery: Separate must-have pages and functions from later ideas. Identify content, approvals, integrations, account access, technical constraints, and client responsibilities.
  3. Direction and proposal: Confirm the recommended approach, page/feature boundaries, ownership, milestones, acceptance criteria, payment terms, exclusions, and any third-party costs before work begins.
  4. Design and build: Develop the agreed page structure and visual direction, with review points and a controlled change process rather than an unlimited revision assumption.
  5. Review and readiness checks: Check agreed links, forms or enquiry handoffs, responsive layouts, content, redirects where relevant, and access/security responsibilities.
  6. Handover: Provide the agreed access and ownership information, deployment details, backup/recovery guidance, outstanding-items list, and support boundary. Any ongoing maintenance is a separate, explicitly scoped decision.

Questions to answer before a proposal

  • What is the business, primary offer, and target customer?
  • Is this a new website, a redesign, a rebuild, or an online store?
  • Which pages and actions are essential at launch?
  • Who supplies and approves copy, images, brand assets, legal text, and proof?
  • Does the business already own the domain, hosting, email, analytics, and third-party accounts?
  • Which payments, bookings, forms, maps, CRM, catalogue, or other integrations are actually required?
  • Who will maintain content, security, hosting, and renewals after handover?
  • What must be explicitly excluded to keep the first release manageable?
  • Who is the decision-maker, what is the target launch condition, and what review capacity is available?
  • If a location is mentioned, is it a genuine service area or a separate physical-location requirement?
