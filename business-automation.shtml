What business automation means

Business automation applies defined steps and rules to parts of a business process. Start with the process as it works today: where it begins, who handles each part, what decisions are made, and where exceptions occur. Those details make it easier to discuss whether automation fits and what needs to be defined before the project is scoped.

A business process automation project may involve more than one task or handoff. Describe the workflow in plain language rather than starting with a tool name or a proposed technical solution. The systems and integrations involved must be checked against the specific project requirements; mentioning a platform here does not mean it is supported.

Bring one workflow to the conversation

A useful first discussion can start with a single workflow. Before you get in touch, consider noting:

  • What starts the process, and what counts as completion?
  • Which people handle the steps, and where do approvals happen?
  • What information is needed at each stage, and where does it come from?
  • Which parts happen in a consistent order, and where do exceptions appear?
  • What existing software is involved?
  • What change would make the process easier to manage?

These details help frame the question. They do not commit either side to a particular tool, integration, implementation method, or outcome.

Set the right boundaries

Some workflows have clear steps. Others depend on changing circumstances, specialist judgment, or information that is difficult to standardize. Those differences matter when deciding what to automate and where a person should remain involved.

It is useful to discuss what happens when information is missing or a step gets stuck, and whether access, checks, or ongoing maintenance need to be part of the scope.

Business automation or AI-agent development?

A predictable process with known steps may be a better fit for business or workflow automation. A task that involves interpreting less structured information may raise a different question: whether an AI agent is appropriate, and what limits it would need. The right choice depends on the task and the confirmed project scope.

Questions about business automation

What kinds of processes can be considered?

Start with a process that has identifiable steps, people, decisions, and exceptions. The exact type of workflow BhaivaTech can take on needs to be confirmed for each enquiry; no particular industry or use case is assumed on this page.

Can you work with our existing software?

Share the names of the systems involved and how they are used in the process. Compatibility, access requirements, and any proposed integration must be checked before they are included in a project scope.

Does every process need automation?

No. First describe the current process and the change you want. If steps or responsibilities are unclear, documenting them may be the useful first move before deciding whether automation is suitable.

What should I include in an enquiry?

Describe the process, the people or approvals involved, the software it touches, and the main difficulty you want to address. You can leave out confidential information until the appropriate handling requirements have been discussed.
